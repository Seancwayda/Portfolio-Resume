AWARDED BID QUALITY ASSURANCE

A contract is only as sound as the estimate behind it.

My review process compared award documents to drawings, specifications, accepted inclusions and exclusions, quoted products, estimated labor, allowances, freight and delivery requirements. I evaluated the exposure of wording that shifted obligations beyond the original scope and coordinated proposed business-side redlines for review.

Contract reviews were commercial/operational evaluations, not the provision of legal counsel.

TYPICAL REVIEW LOOP

From estimate to executable scope

  1. Read the award and contract attachments.
  2. Cross-check included products and quantities against bid records.
  3. Review alternate/VE approvals, addenda and accepted exclusions.
  4. Verify quote age, vendor availability and lead times.
  5. Compare delivery dates, installation sequencing and estimated labor.
  6. Flag discrepancies and document revised terms for decision-makers.
01 / FIELD-TESTED PROCESS

Six checks before the handoff.

Designed around the questions owners, contract reviewers, project managers and operations teams need answered before commitments become problems.

CHECK 01

Award & scope alignment

Is the awarded work consistent with the estimate, assumptions, specifications, inclusions and exclusions?

CHECK 02

Risky contract language

Are there provisions affecting indemnity, schedule exposure, procurement, changes or responsibilities? Flag for authorized legal review.

CHECK 03

Current supplier pricing

Have material quotes expired, changed, or created an opportunity for new negotiated prices?

CHECK 04

Schedule & logistics

Do lead times, installation durations, delivery constraints, labor and site readiness match the promised dates?

CHECK 05

Cost exposure

Are quantities, product substitutions, labor, tax, freight and markups still defensible?

CHECK 06

Document control

Are approved scope, supplier quotations, redlines and handoff files organized, access-controlled and traceable?
